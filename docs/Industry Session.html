Invited Industry Sessions (AusDM'26)

Sydney, Australia | 2–4 December 2026

Invited Industry Sessions

Venue: University of Technology Sydney
Session Chair: Anthony Nolan

The Industry Session brings together experts across various fields to discuss the intersection of Artificial Intelligence, data privacy, ethics, and future technologies.

1. Our Data, Our AI: Privacy in the AI Future

Professor Graham Williams

Presentation: Since the first proposed neural network architecture for AI some 80 years ago developments in Machine Learning and Data Science have supported the emergence of today's useful generative AI models. With the emergence of the world wide web some 30 years ago we were blinded in to giving up volumes of our data to today's big tech who gained incredibly from our data, and certainly more from the data than we have as individuals. In this talk I will introduce the efforts currently underway, together with the inventor of the world wide web Sir Tim Berners-Lee, to facilitate individual engagement in the economy of today's data. We are developing privacy first technology to support the development of AI agents that preserve our privacy yet deliver the gains of AI to us all.

Bio: Professor Graham Williams is Chief Scientist, Software Innovation Institute, Australian National University. Prior to returning to Australia to join the ANU in 2020 Graham was Director of Data Science with Microsoft. Graham has a PhD in Machine Learning and is an Artificial Intelligence developer, researcher, practitioner, and educator as well as an Open Source Software developer, with over 30 years in the industry. He has authored many books and papers as well as a number of popular software packages. As Lead Data Scientist at the Australian Taxation Office from 2004 until 2016 he was involved in setting up the Australian Government’s Data Analytics Centre of Excellence and guided the development of data mining capabilities across the Australian Government. From 1991 to 2004 he was Principal Computer Scientist for Data Mining with the Australian Government’s premier research organisation, CSIRO Australia. He has served as a Senior International Expert and Visiting Professor of the Chinese Academy of Sciences, Adjunct Professor in Data Mining, Fraud Prevention, Security, at the University of Canberra and Adjunct Fellow with the Australian National University. Graham is an active machine learning researcher and regularly teaches courses and maintains resources for the data scientist. Graham’s research has pioneered developments in ensemble learning, outlier detection and profile discovery. He is involved in numerous international artificial intelligence and data mining research activities and conferences and is past-chair of the Pacific Asia Conference on Knowledge Discovery and Data Mining (PAKDD), founder and co-chair of the Australasian Conference on Data Science and Machine Learning (AusDM), and member of the Australasian Artificial Intelligence Conference.

2. Pope Leo’s approach to AI and new technologies

Dr Michael Walker

Presentation: Pope Leo XIV’s encyclical letter, Magnifica Humanitas, published in May, evaluates new technologies including AI from a moral standpoint. The technologies are not inherently moral or immoral, says Leo; rather, the moral imperative is on humans to use them in ways that foster human flourishing instead of ways that foster domination and violence.

Bio: Dr Michael Walker is a Social Justice Facilitator at the Justice and Peace Office of the Catholic Archdiocese of Sydney and a sessional academic teaching Catholic Social Thought at Australian Catholic University. Michael is co-author of Magnificent Humanity: An Australian Group Reading Guide to Pope Leo’s Magnifica Humanitas, published in September by Garratt. His PhD, completed at UTS, was an exploration of the impact on employment relations of digital platforms and algorithmic management.

3. Advancing data privacy for business resilience

Joyce Ho

Presentation: With the growing complexity of the cyber threat landscape, organizations are increasingly investing in both organizational and technical safeguards to protect sensitive information. This presentation will discuss proposed regulatory changes and their significance in fostering stronger data privacy practices and improving overall data governance.

Bio: Joyce serves as a Trust Lead at a global software company. She is also a Master of Research candidate at Charles Darwin University, focusing on the intersection of business continuity, data privacy, and the integration of law and information technology to enhance Australian regulatory frameworks. Her research interests blend her cybersecurity expertise with a passion for improving incident notification regulations and data protection within privacy frameworks. Driven by the challenges of cyber threats and emerging technologies in the digital era, Joyce is committed to proposing regulatory enhancements and promoting cyber resilience across Australia.

4. Air Pocket Spectroscopy: Can We Photograph Air Quality? (Video Presentation)

Ryley Nolan and Flynn Nolan

Description: Can the air above different locations have its own spectral fingerprint? Two young students explored this question using a digital camera, spectroscopic filters and custom Python analysis software. By photographing clear skies across Sydney, they compared spectral patterns from forests, beaches, roads, airports and industrial areas. Their experiments revealed measurable differences between locations and demonstrated the potential for matching unknown locations against a growing library of environmental fingerprints. This youth-led investigation explores how photography, spectroscopy, data science and future machine learning could contribute to environmental monitoring and pollution detection.

Bio: Ryley (year 10) and Flynn (year 8) Nolan are young Australian science innovators and award-winning students from St Ignatius’ College Riverview. They have twice achieved success in the NSW Young Scientist Awards, in 2022 and 2025. Their scientific investigations span water filtration, low-energy water lifting, environmental science and spectroscopy. Their latest project, Air Pocket Spectroscopy, explores how digital photography, spectral analysis and data science can identify environmental differences and potentially contribute to air quality monitoring.

5. Omniunify: Reflecting Forward — Bridging Everything and Anything (Video Presentation)

Anthony (Tony) Nolan OAM

Description: What if we could visualise the connections between everything, whether tangible or intangible, and use those relationships to explore future possibilities? Omniunify is an original conceptual methodology that transforms complex relationships into interactive, multidimensional visualisations, bridging thought, form and action. Using fuzzy logic, interconnected systems and three-dimensional mapping, Omniunify explores how entities, concepts, activities and influences interact across space and time. By examining relationships at micro, meso and macro levels, it provides a new approach to understanding complexity, identifying hidden connections and supporting decisions. This short presentation introduces Omniunify's underlying philosophy and demonstrates how reflecting forward through interconnected information could transform the way we explore, understand and make decisions about our world.

Bio: Anthony (Tony) Nolan OAM is an Australian data scientist, theoretical mathematician, inventor and futurist with over 30 years of experience in intelligence, predictive analytics, behavioural modelling and decision support systems. His work bridges science, technology and innovative thinking. He is the founder of G3N1U5 Pty Ltd and creator of Omniunify, an original systems-of-systems methodology for exploring complex relationships through multidimensional visualisation and fuzzy logic.

6. Panel: The AI Hypothetical: Who Decides, Who Benefits, and Who Is Responsible?

Presented by: IEEE Society on Social Implications of Technology (SSIT) — Australia Chapter

Facilitators: Anthony (Tony) Nolan OAM & Mark Brady (Acting Dean of Law, CDU)

Panel Members: Warwick Graco, Inspector Mathew Mccarthy, and presenters of the Industry section of AusDM2026.

Session Description

As artificial intelligence, data science and machine learning increasingly influence our lives, are we prepared for the decisions, consequences and responsibilities they bring?

This interactive panel session challenges conventional thinking through a series of thought-provoking hypothetical scenarios exploring the opportunities, risks and societal implications of intelligent technologies.

Guided by Anthony Nolan OAM, panellists will examine real-world dilemmas involving human judgement, algorithmic decision-making, privacy, accountability, trust and the evolving relationship between humanity and intelligent machines.

Rather than a traditional panel discussion, this session invites active audience participation, encouraging questions, challenging assumptions and exploring differing perspectives. The objective is not necessarily to find the right answers, but to ask the questions that will shape our technological future.
